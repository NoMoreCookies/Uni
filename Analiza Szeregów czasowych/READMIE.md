# Time Series Analysis

This repository contains materials, notes, and coursework developed for the **Time Series Analysis** course.  
It focuses on the statistical modelling of data observed over time, with emphasis on identifying temporal structures, building forecasting models, and interpreting stochastic processes used in real-world applications.

## Course Overview

The course introduces the fundamental concepts and methods of **time series analysis**, combining theoretical foundations with practical modelling techniques.  
Its main objective is to understand how time-ordered data behave, how to describe their internal structure, and how to construct models for explanation and forecasting.

The main topics covered in this repository include:

- **Introduction to time series**
  - definition and examples of time series data
  - components of a time series: trend, seasonality, cyclicality, and noise
  - visualization and preliminary exploratory analysis

- **Stationarity and dependence structure**
  - weak stationarity
  - autocovariance and autocorrelation
  - partial autocorrelation
  - white noise processes

- **Classical time series models**
  - autoregressive models (**AR**)
  - moving average models (**MA**)
  - autoregressive moving average models (**ARMA**)
  - autoregressive integrated moving average models (**ARIMA**)

- **Model identification and estimation**
  - interpretation of ACF and PACF plots
  - order selection
  - parameter estimation
  - diagnostic checking of residuals

- **Forecasting**
  - point forecasts
  - forecast errors
  - model comparison
  - evaluation of predictive performance

- **Practical applications**
  - economic and financial time series
  - trend and volatility interpretation
  - short-term and long-term forecasting

## Learning Goals

The purpose of this repository is to organize the key concepts discussed during the course and provide worked examples related to time series modelling.  
In particular, the course develops the ability to:

- analyze the structure of time-dependent data,
- determine whether a series is stationary,
- interpret autocorrelation patterns,
- build and compare AR, MA, ARMA, and ARIMA models,
- assess model adequacy using residual diagnostics,
- generate and evaluate forecasts.

## Repository Content

The repository may include:

- lecture notes,
- problem sets,
- R Markdown files,
- numerical examples,
- model implementations,
- plots and visualizations of time series data.

## Summary

Overall, this course provides a solid introduction to the statistical tools used in **time series modelling and forecasting**.  
It builds the foundation for analyzing dynamic phenomena observed over time, especially in economics, finance, and data analysis.
